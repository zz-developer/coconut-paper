\paragraph{Explicit Reasoning Models and Large-scale Reinforcement Learning}

Advances in \textit{explicit} reasoning models, including OpenAI o1 \& o3~\citep{openai2025o3o4mini, openai2024o1} and DeepSeek R1~\citep{deepseekai2025deepseekr1incentivizingreasoningcapability}, demonstrated their strong ability in mathematical reasoning.
Their work mainly focused on scaling up test-time computation by generating long Chain of Thought (CoT)~~\citep{DBLP:journals/corr/abs-2201-11903} text sequences including symbolic inference and reflection.

Advances in these models can't leave out the strong power of large-scale reinforcement learning paradigm, which has been widely used in LLMs.
\citet{ouyang2022traininglanguagemodelsfollow} showed how GPT-3~\citep{kojima2023largelanguagemodelszeroshot} can be fine-tuned with reinforcement learning via Proximal Policy Optimization (PPO)~\citet{DBLP:journals/corr/SchulmanWDRK17}, which optimizes the agent's policy by maximizing the expected reward while ensuring the new policy does not deviate excessively from the old policy.
\citet{shao2024deepseekmathpushinglimitsmathematical} Proposed Group Relative Policy Optimization (GRPO), which overcame the limitations of PPO, including the need for a large number of samples and the difficulty of tuning hyperparameters.
\citet{yu2025dapoopensourcellmreinforcement} Demonstrated the effectiveness of Decoupled Clip and Dynamic Sampling Policy Optimization (DAPO) in large-scale reinforcement learning, which performed best in several benchmarks on Qwen2.5-32B~\citet{qwen2.5} solely based on pure reinforcement learning and the base instruction model of Qwen2.5-32B~\citep{qwen2.5}.

In contrast, our work leverages a latent space instead of explicit reasoning text, which reduces verbosity and improves efficiency.
Based on large-scale reinforcement learning, we effectively transplant those explicit reasoning-based optimization and advances into the latent reasoning region.

\paragraph{Latent Space Reasoning}
Unlike explicit reasoning, latent reasoning paradigms leverage latent spaces, promoting efficiency and accuracy.
Previous works have already found that LLMs may involve hidden computation steps, which are not explicitly generated in text~\citep{yang2024largelanguagemodelslatently, chen2023theoremqatheoremdrivenquestionanswering}.
Driven by this spirit, \citet{hao2024traininglargelanguagemodels}proposed \textsc{Coconut}, assigning models to ``reason in a latent continuous space'' through specifically designed training algorithms, and demonstrated its potential compared to the explicit Chain-of-Thought reasoning procedure, especially on tasks requiring backtracking.

\citet{geiping2025scalingtesttimecomputelatent} Introduced a new recurrent transformer architecture that lets models perform multiple hidden reasoning iterations per token, as they suggested that ``a recurrent block can be looped arbitrarily at inference to refine the hidden state before producing the next token.''
\citet{saunshi2025reasoninglatentthoughtspower} Demonstrated that the critical impact of model capabilities lies in its depth instead of parameter size.
In this way, they proposed \textit{Looped Transformer}, which generated latent thoughts and simulated Chain-of-Thought reasoning procedures internally.
They also pretrained a 3.5B model that might compete with 50B ones.

In contrast, our work focuses on the practical implementation of latent reasoning, instead of cold-start training, which is done by~\citeauthor{saunshi2025reasoninglatentthoughtspower}.
Our paradigm for feasibility and stability is based on the approach of training the model without specifically designed trajectories, such as stepping guidance, from a production-level model via small injections with VAE and gates.
It also combines the advances of explicit reasoning models and large-scale reinforcement learning objectives.
At the same time, prior work mainly focused on producing the latent reasoning space, and small-scale usages primarily based on SFT\@.
