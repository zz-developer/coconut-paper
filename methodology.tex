In this section, describe our training paradigm that leverages the Reinforcement Learning (RL) paradigm to solidify the \emph{Soft Thinking}~\citep{zhang2025softthinkingunlockingreasoning} paradigm, which uses concept tokens to represent the latent reasoning steps of the model, retaining the full probabilities of tokens rather than decoding to a discrete one.

\subsection{Modified Soft Thinking}\label{subsec:modified-soft-thinking}

\paragraph{Preliminaries.}

We briefly review core concepts of Soft Thinking proposed
by \citeauthor{zhang2025softthinkingunlockingreasoning}, which serves the foundation of our work.

Let $ct \coloneqq p \in \Delta^{\left\lvert V\right\rvert - 1}$ denotes the ``probability distribution over the vocabulary'' at any intermediate step
(i.e., reasoning step) of the language model, which may allow the model to explore different paths simultaneously.
By convexly combining all embedding vectors, as well as concept tokens here, we can obtain the ``continuous concept space,'' as denoted in~\ref{eq:concept-space}

\begin{equation}
    \mathcal{C} \;=\;
    \Bigl\{\,\sum\nolimits_{k=1}^{|V|}\alpha_k\,e(k)\;:\;
    \alpha\in\Delta^{|V|-1}\Bigr\}\subset\mathbb{R}^d ,
    \label{eq:concept-space}
\end{equation}

At each step, the model can produce a concept token rather than explicit decoded token, and we can inject the concept token back into the model, as denoted in~\ref{eq:soft-thinking-concept-token}.

\begin{equation}
    \tilde{e}_{\mathrm{next}}
    = \sum_{k=1}^{|V|} ct[k]\,e(k) = \sum_{k=1}^{|V|} p[k]\,e(k)\in\mathcal{C}.
    \label{eq:soft-thinking-concept-token}
\end{equation}

\paragraph{Refinement.}

We fortuitously found that in the process of computing concept probabilities, changing the order of applying Softmax
and then gather the logits and apply Softmax, which can slightly improve the model's performance.

By applying Softmax later, the numerical stability holds better and reduces extreme parameters, which can eventually
result to better and more stable reasoning procedures.

\subsection{Reinforcement Learning}\label{subsec:reinforcement-learning}

\paragraph{Algorithm.} We adopt the Clipped IS-weight Policy Optimization (CISPO)~\citep{minimax2025minimaxm1scalingtesttimecompute} algorithm
in our pipeline, which can be denoted in~\ref{eq:CISPO}.

\begin{equation}
    \begin{aligned}
    \mathcal{J}_{\mathrm{CISPO}}(\theta) &= \mathbb{E}_{(q,a) \sim \mathcal{D}, \{o_i\}_{i=1}^G \sim \pi_{\theta_{\text{old}}}(\cdot|q)} \\
    & \left[
        \frac{1}{\sum_{i=1}^G |o_i|} \sum_{i=1}^G \sum_{t=1}^{|o_i|}
        \texttt{sg}(\hat{r}_{i,t}(\theta))\hat{A}_{i,t}\log \pi_\theta(o_{i,t} \mid q, o_{i,<t})
    \right],
    \end{aligned}
    \label{eq:CISPO}
\end{equation}
where $\texttt{sg}(\cdot)$ denotes the stop-gradient operation, and $\hat{r}_{i,t}(\theta)$ is the clipped IS weight:

\begin{equation*}
    \hat{r}_{i,t}(\theta) = \text{clip}\left(r_{
    i,t}(\theta), 1-\epsilon^{IS}_{low}, 1+\epsilon^{IS}_{high}\right).
\end{equation*}

\paragraph{Reward.} We verify answers~\citep{Kydlicek_Math-Verify_Math_Verification} that is wrapped with \texttt{\textbackslash boxed} block, and set the reward as follows:

\begin{equation*}
    r = \begin{cases}
        2, & \mathrm{if\ correct} \\
        -1, & \mathrm{if\ incorrect}
    \end{cases}
\end{equation*}

\subsubsection{Swapping}\label{subsubsec:swapping}

In the rollout stage of the GRPO~\citep{shao2024deepseekmathpushinglimitsmathematical} algorithm and its followers such
as DAPO~\citep{yu2025dapoopensourcellmreinforcement} and CISPO~\citep{minimax2025minimaxm1scalingtesttimecompute},
to each prompt the algorithms require sampling multiple answers, i.e., doing group sampling, which is a crucial factor of
more accurate advantage estimation.
Latest value-model-based algorithms, including PPO~\citep{DBLP:journals/corr/SchulmanWDRK17} leveraged in
Open-Reasoner-Zero~\citep{hu2025openreasonerzeroopensourceapproach} and
VAPO~\citep{yue2025vapoefficientreliablereinforcement}, recommend applying group sampling for performance enhancement.

However, just as the Greedy Sampling, the original Soft Thinking sampling holds no diversity, which means that multiple
sampling will only gain one unique answer, thus making group sampling impossible.

To unlock the power of group sampling, we propose a ``swapping'' technique to add diversity to the sampling process.
Specifically, for a output logits tensor $\boldsymbol{l_{t}}$ on the $t$-th token, perform the following
computation:

\begin{gather*}
    \boldsymbol{p} = \mathrm{Softmax}(\boldsymbol{l}_{t+1} / T, \mathrm{dim}=-1) \\
    \mathrm{topk\_probs}, \mathrm{topk\_indices} =
    \mathrm{Topk}(\boldsymbol{p}, k=10) \\
    \mathrm{token}_{t+1} = \mathrm{MultiNomial}(\mathrm{topk\_probs})
\end{gather*}
